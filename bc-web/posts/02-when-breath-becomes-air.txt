title: “when breath becomes air”

> “You can’t ever reach perfection, but you can believe in an asymptote toward which you are ceaselessly striving.”
> ~ Paul Kalanithi

When Breath Becomes Air grapples with the harsh reality of becoming a “dual-citizen” of the physical and spiritual world. Paul’s lung cancer diagnosis weighed down his body on Earth, yet liberated his spirit and peace more than anything he’d ever experienced. Daunting chemotherapy sessions and seething limbic pain never deterred Paul Kalanithi from playing his part as an excellent neurosurgeon. Despite the suffering, he strove ceaselessly. This, above all, is what Paul did, even as his health diminished. Interestingly, the memoir does not revolve around his desire to “beat” cancer or fully recover; Paul approached his diagnosis pragmatically, wishing only to fill what little of his days remained with a sweltering purpose to aspire, reflect, and love. Seeing his rational perspectives with lung cancer, rather than the usual cancer patient’s overly-idealized hopes of a cancer-free future, is both refreshing and heartbreaking. It seems that this is what working in the medical field does to a person.

Human life is fleeting. But Paul has urged me to strive ceaselessly in these passing moments, grasping the pastimes and people that fill my cup with warmth, before it’s too late to do so. Death ends lives, but only in dying do we truly begin to live. I refuse to wait for Death’s arrival before I begin to relentlessly pursue purpose.
